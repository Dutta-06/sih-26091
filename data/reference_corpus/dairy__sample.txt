Dairy - small-holder milch animal units. A common rural micro-enterprise
model involves 2-5 crossbred milch animals, with capital covering animal
purchase, a basic shed, and initial fodder stock. Viable sub-niches
typically include raw milk supply to a local dairy cooperative
collection point, small-batch curd/paneer production sold directly to
nearby households and shops, and door-step milk delivery in villages
without an organized collection route. Saturation in a given locality
depends heavily on whether a cooperative collection point already exists
nearby and how many other households in the same radius already keep
milch animals for sale rather than only for household consumption.

Dairy - fodder and feed supply. A secondary niche adjacent to dairy
farming is organized fodder cultivation and feed-mix supply to other
dairy farmers in the same cluster, which tends to be less saturated in
areas where dairy farming itself is common but fodder is still
individually sourced.
